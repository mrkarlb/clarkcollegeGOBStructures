# Start Here {#start}

A molecule's structure decides what it does. Carbon monoxide is poisonous because of where its electrons sit. Bicarbonate can carry carbon dioxide through your blood because its charge is spread over three oxygens. Water dissolves salts, sugars, and many medicines because of its shape. Every one of those explanations starts with a **Lewis structure**: a drawing that shows every atom, every bond, and every lone pair of electrons in a molecule.

This page walks through drawing Lewis structures step by step, then uses them to explain charges and resonance. The second page, [Shape, Polarity, and Intermolecular Forces](shape.html), builds from there: a molecule's Lewis structure sets its shape, its shape and bonds set its polarity, and polarity sets how molecules attract each other.

## How to use this site

- **Work through the sections in order.** Each one uses the skills from the last.
- **Draw along.** Lewis structures are a skill, like reading a drug label. Keep paper next to you and draw each example before you open "Walk through it."
- **Try the practice problems before opening the answer.** Struggling for a minute is how the steps stick.
- **Use the model kits in lab.** The drawings here are flat. The kits show you how the same molecules look in three dimensions.

## Reading the drawings

Every atom is shown with its symbol. Lines are bonds: one line for a single bond (one shared pair of electrons), two for a double bond, three for a triple bond. **Pairs of dots are lone pairs**, electrons that belong to one atom and aren't shared. A small **+** or **−** next to an atom is its formal charge. An ion is drawn inside square brackets, with its overall charge at the top right.

Colors help you find atoms quickly: oxygen is red, nitrogen blue, and fluorine and chlorine green. Each drawing also has a text description for screen readers. Use the search box to find any molecule or term, and the dark mode button if you study at night.
