# Drawing Lewis Structures {#drawing}

Drawing Lewis structures takes practice, but the same steps work every time. Have the Lewis symbol for each atom ready first (see [Lewis Symbols](#symbols)). We'll use formaldehyde, CH₂O, as the example throughout.

## Before you start: count the electrons

Add up the valence electrons of every atom in the formula. This is your budget: the finished structure has to show exactly this many electrons, with **every bond counting as two**.

[[count ch2o]]

For an ion, adjust the count for the charge: **add** one electron for each negative charge, and **subtract** one for each positive charge.

## The three steps

1. **Choose the central atom and connect every atom to it with a single bond.** The central atom is the one that makes the most bonds, which is usually the one with the most single electrons. It is usually written first in the formula and is usually the least electronegative element present, not counting H. **H is never central**, because it can make only one bond. For a molecule with more than one carbon, connect the carbons to each other first, then attach the other atoms around that carbon backbone.
2. **Add each atom's remaining electrons, as pairs and single electrons.** Start from each atom's Lewis symbol and remove one single electron for every bond you drew in Step 1. Single electrons left over show where more bonds are still needed; double and triple bonds tend to make themselves known at this point.
3. **Pair up single electrons on neighboring atoms to make double or triple bonds.** Leave lone pairs alone. If an atom is still short of an octet after this, a neighbor has to share a lone pair; that case is covered in [Bonding Patterns](#patterns).

[[steps ch2o]]

<details class="walk" markdown="1">
<summary>Walk through it</summary>

1. **Count:** {{ve ch2o}} valence electrons.
2. **Step 1:** C makes the most bonds (it has four single electrons), so C goes in the center, with the two H and the O attached by single bonds.
3. **Step 2:** C started with four single electrons and used three of them, so it has one left. O started with two pairs and two single electrons and used one single electron, so it has two pairs and one single electron.
4. **Step 3:** The single electron on C pairs with the single electron on O, making a second C–O bond: **C=O**. O's two pairs stay as lone pairs.
5. **Check:** each H has 2 electrons; C has four bonds (8 electrons); O has a double bond and two lone pairs (8 electrons). Electrons shown: two C–H bonds (4) + one C=O double bond (4) + two lone pairs (4) = 12, which matches the count.
</details>

<div class="note" markdown="1">
**Don't leave out the lone pairs.** Lone pairs affect a molecule's [shape](shape.html#shape), its bond angles, and how it interacts with other molecules. A Lewis structure isn't finished until every lone pair is drawn.
</div>

## Check every structure

When you finish, run three checks:

- **The electron total matches your count.** Count 2 for every bond line and 2 for every lone pair.
- **Every H has 2 electrons**: exactly one bond and no lone pairs.
- **Every C, N, O, and F has exactly 8 electrons**, counting each bond line as 2. This is the **octet rule**. C, N, O, and F never have more than 8.

## More worked examples

### Water, ammonia, and methane

[[lewisrow h2o nh3 ch4]]

<details class="walk" markdown="1">
<summary>Walk through it</summary>

1. **Water:** {{ve h2o}} valence electrons. O has two single electrons, so it makes two bonds, one to each H. Its two pairs stay as lone pairs.
2. **Ammonia:** {{ve nh3}} valence electrons. N has three single electrons, so it bonds to three H and keeps one lone pair.
3. **Methane:** {{ve ch4}} valence electrons. C has four single electrons and bonds to four H, with no lone pairs.
4. **Check:** in each molecule, the central atom has 8 electrons and each H has 2. These three show the bond counts from the Lewis symbols in action: O makes 2 bonds, N makes 3, and C makes 4.
</details>

### Carbon dioxide: two double bonds

[[steps co2]]

<details class="walk" markdown="1">
<summary>Walk through it</summary>

1. **Count:** {{ve co2}} valence electrons.
2. **Step 1:** C makes the most bonds, so C goes in the center: O–C–O.
3. **Step 2:** C used two of its four single electrons, so it has two left. Each O used one of its two single electrons, so each O has one single electron and two pairs.
4. **Step 3:** each O pairs its single electron with one of C's, making **two C=O double bonds**.
5. **Check:** C has two double bonds (8 electrons); each O has a double bond and two lone pairs (8 electrons). Electrons shown: two double bonds (8) + four lone pairs (8) = 16.
</details>

### Hydrogen cyanide: a triple bond

[[steps hcn]]

<details class="walk" markdown="1">
<summary>Walk through it</summary>

1. **Count:** {{ve hcn}} valence electrons.
2. **Step 1:** H can't be central, so C goes in the middle: H–C–N.
3. **Step 2:** C used two of its four single electrons and has two left. N used one of its three single electrons, so it has two single electrons and one pair.
4. **Step 3:** both of C's single electrons pair with N's two single electrons, making two more C–N bonds: a **triple bond**, C≡N.
5. **Check:** C has one single bond and one triple bond (8 electrons); N has a triple bond and one lone pair (8 electrons). Electrons shown: C–H (2) + C≡N (6) + one lone pair (2) = 10.
</details>

### Ethene: more than one carbon

[[steps c2h4]]

<details class="walk" markdown="1">
<summary>Walk through it</summary>

1. **Count:** {{ve c2h4}} valence electrons.
2. **Step 1:** with two carbons, connect them first, C–C. Then attach two H to each C.
3. **Step 2:** each C has made three bonds, so each has one single electron left.
4. **Step 3:** the two single electrons pair up, making a **C=C double bond**.
5. **Check:** each C has four bonds (8 electrons). Electrons shown: four C–H bonds (8) + C=C (4) = 12.
</details>
