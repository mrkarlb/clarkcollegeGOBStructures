# Lewis Symbols {#symbols}

Before you can draw a molecule, you need the **Lewis symbol** for each atom in it: the element's symbol surrounded by one dot for each **valence electron**, the outermost electrons that take part in bonding.

## Counting valence electrons

For the main-group elements, the number of valence electrons comes straight from the periodic table. It's the group number for groups 1 and 2, and the last digit of the group number for groups 13 through 18. Hydrogen has 1, carbon 4, nitrogen 5, oxygen 6, and fluorine 7.

## Drawing the symbol

Picture four sides around the element symbol: top, right, bottom, left. Place the dots **one per side first**, then go around again to make pairs.

[[symbols H C N O F]]

<div class="note" markdown="1">
**The key idea.** The **single electrons** are where bonds form. Each single electron can pair with a single electron from another atom to make one bond. The **pairs** usually stay as they are, as **lone pairs**. So the number of single electrons tells you how many bonds an atom usually makes.
</div>

| Atom | Valence electrons | Single electrons | Usual number of bonds | Lone pairs left |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| C | 4 | 4 | 4 | 0 |
| N | 5 | 3 | 3 | 1 |
| O | 6 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| F, Cl | 7 | 1 | 1 | 3 |

A quick way to remember the bond counts: **H**, **O**, **N**, **C** make **1**, **2**, **3**, **4** bonds.

## Same group, same dots

Elements in the same group have the same number of valence electrons, so their Lewis symbols look alike. Sulfur draws like oxygen, phosphorus like nitrogen, and chlorine like fluorine.

[[symbols P S Cl]]

Atoms of these larger elements usually follow the same bond counts as the element above them. They can also make more bonds than that; [Beyond the Octet](shape.html#octet) explains when.
