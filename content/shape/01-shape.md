# Molecular Shape {#shape}

Electrons repel each other. The groups of electrons around a central atom spread out until they are as far apart as possible, and that spreading sets the molecule's shape. This idea is called **VSEPR** (valence shell electron pair repulsion).

## Counting electron groups

Look at the central atom in the Lewis structure and count its **electron groups**:

- **Every atom bonded to it counts as one group**, whether the bond is single, double, or triple.
- **Every lone pair on it counts as one group.**

## Two geometries

The groups decide two related shapes:

- **Electron geometry** is the arrangement of *all* the electron groups, lone pairs included.
- **Molecular geometry** is the arrangement of the *atoms* only. This is the shape of the molecule itself, and it's what "shape" means unless a question says otherwise.

When the central atom has no lone pairs, the two are the same. When it has lone pairs, they aren't: the lone pairs still take up space, but you can't "see" them in the shape.

[[shapetable co2 ch2o o3a ch4 nh3 h2o]]

<div class="note" markdown="1">
**Lone pairs squeeze the bond angles.** A lone pair is held by only one atom, so it spreads out more than a bonding pair and pushes the bonds closer together. That's why the angle drops from 109.5° in CH₄ to about 107° in NH₃ (one lone pair) and about 104.5° in H₂O (two lone pairs), even though all three have four electron groups.
</div>

## Worked examples

[[shaperow ch2o h2o]]

<details class="walk" markdown="1">
<summary>Walk through it</summary>

1. **Formaldehyde, CH₂O:** C is the central atom. It is bonded to two H and one O, and has no lone pairs: **3 electron groups, 0 lone pairs**. The C=O double bond counts as one group. Electron geometry and molecular geometry are both **trigonal planar**, with 120° bond angles.
2. **Water, H₂O:** O is the central atom. It is bonded to two H and has two lone pairs: **4 electron groups, 2 lone pairs**. The electron geometry is **tetrahedral**, but the molecular geometry, the shape of the atoms, is **bent**.
3. **The difference:** with no lone pairs on the central atom (CH₂O), electron and molecular geometry match. With lone pairs (H₂O), they don't.
</details>

<div class="note" markdown="1">
**Molecules with more than one central atom.** In a molecule with a carbon backbone, find the shape around **each** atom that is bonded to two or more others. In ethanol, CH₃CH₂OH, both carbons are tetrahedral, and the O, with two bonds and two lone pairs, is bent.
</div>
