# Start Here {#overview}

A Lewis structure is flat, but molecules aren't. This page takes the Lewis structures from [the first page](index.html) and follows them through to what a molecule actually does: its **shape**, whether it is **polar**, and how strongly it **attracts other molecules**. Those attractions decide whether a substance is a gas or a liquid, whether it dissolves in water, and how a medicine travels through your blood.

[[roadmap]]

You'll need a correct Lewis structure for every step here, with every lone pair drawn. If a structure gives you trouble, go back to [Drawing Lewis Structures](index.html#drawing).

## Reading the shape drawings

Shapes are drawn with three kinds of bonds. A **plain line** lies flat in the page. A **solid wedge** points out of the page, toward you. A **dashed bond** points into the page, away from you. Lone pairs on the central atom are shown as pairs of dots; lone pairs on the outer atoms are left off so the shape is easier to see.
