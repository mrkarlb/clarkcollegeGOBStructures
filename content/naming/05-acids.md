# Acids {#acids}

An **acid** releases H⁺ ions in water. The acids in this course are H⁺ combined with an anion, so the formula starts with H and **the acid's name comes from the anion's name**. You'll study how acids behave later in the course; this section is here so the names make sense when you get there.

## The three rules

| Anion ending | Acid name | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-ide** | **hydro- … -ic acid** | chloride → hydrochloric acid |
| **-ate** | **-ic acid** | nitrate → nitric acid |
| **-ite** | **-ous acid** | nitrite → nitrous acid |

A memory aid: "I *ate* something *ic*-ky, and *it* was delici-*ous*" (-ate → -ic, -ite → -ous). **Add one H⁺ for each negative charge** on the anion.

[[acids cl br no3 no2 so4 so3 po4 co3 ac]]

**Sulfur and phosphorus add a syllable:** sulfate gives **sulfuric** acid (not "sulfic"), and phosphate gives **phosphoric** acid.

## The oxyacids of chlorine

The per- and hypo- prefixes carry straight over from the [ions](#polyatomic) to the acids.

<div class="health" markdown="1">
**Acids in the body.** Your stomach makes **hydrochloric acid** to digest food. **Carbonic acid** forms when CO₂ dissolves in your blood and is the partner of bicarbonate in the blood's main buffer. **Phosphoric acid** gives colas their tang. **Acetic acid** is the acid in vinegar.
</div>
