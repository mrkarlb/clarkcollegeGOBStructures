# Naming Ionic Compounds {#ionic}

An ionic compound has **no overall charge**: the positive and negative charges cancel. That one rule is all you need to write a formula, and it's how you find a Roman numeral when you name one.

## From name to formula

1. **Write each ion with its charge.** Use the [charts](#ions) for monatomic and [polyatomic](#polyatomic) ions.
2. **Balance the charges.** Find the smallest number of each ion that makes the total zero.
3. **Write the formula** with the cation first. The numbers of each ion become subscripts, and a subscript of 1 isn't written. **If you need more than one of a polyatomic ion, put it in parentheses** with the number outside.

[[ionic na-cl ca-cl al-o2 mg-n3 ca-po4 nh4-so4 al-so4]]

<details class="walk" markdown="1">
<summary>Walk through it: calcium phosphate</summary>

1. **Ions:** calcium is Ca²⁺; phosphate is PO₄³⁻.
2. **Balance:** 2 and 3 meet at 6. Three Ca²⁺ make +6; two PO₄³⁻ make −6.
3. **Formula:** phosphate is polyatomic and you need two, so it goes in parentheses: **Ca₃(PO₄)₂**. Without the parentheses, Ca₃PO₄₂ would mean 42 oxygens.
</details>

<div class="warn" markdown="1">
**Don't change the ion to make the charges work.** SO₄²⁻ is always SO₄²⁻. You balance charges by changing **how many** of each ion there are, never what's inside the ion.
</div>

## From formula to name

1. **Name the cation.** If it's a metal with more than one common charge, you'll need a Roman numeral (step 3).
2. **Name the anion:** -ide for a single atom, or the polyatomic ion's name.
3. **Find the metal's charge, if needed.** The anions' total negative charge must be canceled by the metals. Divide by the number of metal atoms.

**Ionic names never use prefixes.** CaCl₂ is calcium chloride, not calcium dichloride. The charges already tell you how many of each ion there are.

[[ionic fe3-o2 fe2-so4 cu1-cl cu2-so4 sn2-f pb4-o2 hg1-cl]]

<details class="walk" markdown="1">
<summary>Walk through it: Fe₂O₃</summary>

1. **Cation:** iron, which can be Fe²⁺ or Fe³⁺, so the name needs a Roman numeral.
2. **Anion:** O is oxide, O²⁻.
3. **Charge:** three O²⁻ make −6. Two Fe have to make +6, so each Fe is +3. The name is **iron(III) oxide**, not "diiron trioxide." It's ionic, so no prefixes.
</details>

<div class="health" markdown="1">
**Ionic compounds in the clinic.** Lactated Ringer's, an IV fluid, contains sodium chloride, potassium chloride, and calcium chloride. Barium ions are toxic, yet patients drink **barium sulfate** before some X-rays: BaSO₄ is so insoluble that almost no Ba²⁺ dissolves, and the dense barium outlines the digestive tract. **Calcium oxalate** is the most common kind of kidney stone.
</div>
