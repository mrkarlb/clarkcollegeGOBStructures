# Ions {#ions}

An **ion** is an atom or group of atoms with a charge. Atoms gain or lose electrons to end up with the same number as the nearest noble gas.

- **Metals lose electrons** and become **positive ions (cations)**. A cation keeps the element's name: Na⁺ is the *sodium ion*.
- **Nonmetals gain electrons** and become **negative ions (anions)**. An anion takes the ending **-ide**: Cl⁻ is *chloride*, O²⁻ is *oxide*.

## Main-group ions: the group tells you the charge

[[ionchart main]]

Groups 1 and 2 lose 1 and 2 electrons. Aluminum, in group 13, loses 3. Nonmetals gain enough electrons to reach 8: group 17 gains 1, group 16 gains 2, and group 15 gains 3.

<div class="health" markdown="1">
**Electrolytes.** Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, and Cl⁻ are the electrolytes measured in a basic blood panel. Na⁺ and Cl⁻ are the main ions outside your cells; K⁺ is the main ion inside them. Normal saline, the most common IV fluid, is 0.9% sodium chloride.
</div>

## Transition metals: the Roman numeral gives the charge

Most transition metals can form **more than one** ion. Iron can be Fe²⁺ or Fe³⁺, so "iron oxide" doesn't tell you which. The name includes the charge as a **Roman numeral in parentheses**, with no space: **iron(III)** is Fe³⁺.

[[ionchart variable]]

<div class="note" markdown="1">
**Older names.** Before Roman numerals, the lower charge ended in **-ous** and the higher one in **-ic**: ferrous (Fe²⁺) and ferric (Fe³⁺). You'll still see them on supplement labels and in older references, and occasionally in online homework. *Stannous fluoride* in toothpaste is tin(II) fluoride.
</div>

**Mercury(I) is unusual:** it's Hg₂²⁺, two mercury atoms bonded together, each +1. It always travels as a pair, so mercury(I) chloride is Hg₂Cl₂.

A few transition metals have **only one** common charge. They don't get a Roman numeral; writing one is incorrect.

## Diatomic elements

Seven elements exist as **two-atom molecules** when they aren't in a compound: **H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, and I₂**. They're named just like the element: *oxygen*, *chlorine*.
